01 / The Adversary

What your walls are actually up against

Calgary sits over a kilometre above sea level on open prairie. That altitude and that exposure produce a specific kind of damage — and it is almost never the damage homeowners think they're looking at.

+20°0°−20°−40°

Chinook · +9 °C

The freeze–thaw machine

Snowmelt seeps into a hairline crack. A chinook lifts the temperature and pushes that water deeper. Overnight it drops back below zero — and water expands roughly 9% when it freezes, levering the crack wider from the inside.

One cycle does nothing you'd notice. Calgary runs this loop dozens of times each winter. A crack you could barely see in October can be a hole in April.

Drag the slider, or just watch — the wall runs the cycle for you.

  • T‑01

    Freeze–thaw

    Water gets in, freezes, expands, and levers the opening wider. Repeat all winter. This is the single biggest cause of stucco failure in this city.

    Tell: hairline cracks that grow each spring.

  • T‑02

    Chinook swing

    Twenty degrees in an hour. Everything on your wall expands and contracts faster than most materials were engineered to — so the weakest joint gives first.

    Tell: cracks at window corners and control joints.

  • T‑03

    Prairie hail

    Golf‑ball to tennis‑ball, June through August. It pits and chips acrylic finishes and can punch clean through the soft foam substrate of an EIFS wall.

    Tell: pockmarks on the storm‑facing elevation.

  • T‑04

    Wind‑driven rain

    Gusts well over 80 km/h on open lots and acreages drive water sideways into joints, flashing and window heads. Gravity never gets a chance to help.

    Tell: dark streaks or staining below windows.

  • T‑05

    Woodpeckers

    An EIFS wall sounds hollow. To a woodpecker that reads as a dead tree full of insects, or a good place to nest. One bird can open a wall in a weekend.

    Tell: clean round holes, 3–6 cm, high on the wall.

  • T‑06

    Efflorescence

    That chalky white bloom isn't dirt. It's mineral salt carried out of the wall by water that shouldn't be in there — a symptom, and a useful one.

    Tell: white powder near grade or below flashing.

    Traditional vs. EIFS — the honest version

    Neither one is "better." They fail differently, cost differently and repair differently. This is what actually separates them.

    FactorTraditionalEIFS
    What it isSand, cement, lime & water over wire lath — three coatsRigid foam board, mesh‑reinforced base coat, acrylic finish
    Upfront costLower material cost, higher labour (multi‑day, multi‑coat)Higher — insulation board plus specialised install
    InsulationEffectively none on its ownContinuous foam layer — real energy savings
    Impact / hailDense and rigid. Shrugs off most debrisSoft substrate. Dents, cracks and punctures
    MoistureSlightly porous — trapped water can escapeNeeds a drainage cavity. Old barrier systems trap water
    WoodpeckersRarely a targetSounds hollow. Prime target
    RepairStraightforward for a good crew — matching texture and colour is the real skillMust be done by an EIFS‑experienced crew or the patch ruins the drainage plane

    If your home has EIFS: confirm any repair uses a modern drainage‑style assembly, not the outdated barrier system. A patch that seals water in causes far more damage than the hole it covered.

    04 / Field Notes

    Plain answers, no sales pitch

    The four things homeowners ask us most, explained the way we'd explain them standing on your driveway.

    Explainer

    What is parging, in sixty seconds

    Parging is a thin cement coat troweled onto the exposed part of your concrete foundation — the strip between the ground and where your siding or stucco starts.

    1. Why it existsBare concrete block and poured foundation are porous. Parging is the sacrificial skin that takes the weather instead of the structure.
    2. What kills itWater pooling at grade, then freezing. It lifts the coat off the concrete underneath — which is why parging fails in sheets, not spots.
    3. How it's done rightChip off everything loose, wash it, and bond a fresh premium mix to clean concrete. Skipping prep is why the last one fell off.
    4. How you'll knowFlaking, hollow‑sounding patches, crumbling corners, or a visible gap where the coat has pulled away from the wall.

    Explainer

    Why woodpeckers pick your house

    It isn't random and it isn't personal. An EIFS wall is foam over an air gap, so it sounds exactly like a hollow, insect‑ridden tree when tapped. The bird is doing the right thing to the wrong object.

    That's also why patching alone fails. If the wall still sounds hollow, the bird comes back — usually within metres of the last hole. Repair fixes the damage; deterrents fix the cause.

    Explainer

    The white powder isn't dirt

    It's efflorescence — mineral salt that was dissolved inside your wall, carried to the surface by moving water, and left behind when the water evaporated.

    Scrubbing it off treats the stain, not the leak. The useful information is where it appears: below a window means flashing, near grade means drainage, mid‑wall means something behind the finish is wet.

    Checklist

    When to actually call someone

    Not every crack is urgent. These are, and they get worse fast in this climate.

    • Multiple cracks, or one that widened over a single winter
    • Water stains or dark streaks, especially under windows
    • Stucco that sounds hollow or moves when pressed
    • Bulging, delamination, or crumbling corners
    • White powdery bloom near grade
    • Anything after a major hailstorm — even if it looks fine

    Inspect your exterior once a year, and again after any serious hail. Repair cracks when they appear — that's the whole maintenance program.

    Straight talk

    Questions you should ask any exterior contractor

    Including us. If a contractor gets defensive at any of these, that's your answer.

    • Are you licensed and insured — and can I see proof of WCB coverage?
    • How much of your work has been in Calgary's freeze–thaw conditions?
    • Can I see recent, comparable local work and speak to those owners?
    • What exactly is in the written quote — scope, materials, timeline, payment schedule, warranty?
    • How will you diagnose the cause, not just patch the symptom?
    • What does your site protection and daily cleanup look like?

    05 / The Method

    How a job actually runs

    Six stages. Nothing exotic — just the order that stops a repair from failing again in eighteen months.

    1. 01

      Look, and probe

      Free on‑site assessment. We read the crack pattern, check window flashing, caulking and gutters, and probe for moisture behind the finish.

    2. 02

      Find the cause

      Drainage, flashing, foundation movement or plain bad install. Patching a symptom is how a crack returns within a year or two.

    3. 03

      Quote it in writing

      Scope, materials, timeline and price on paper before anything starts. Free, no obligation, and no surprises at the end.

    4. 04

      Strip and prepare

      Remove everything loose or deteriorated down to sound substrate, repair the wall beneath if water got there, and clean for adhesion.

    5. 05

      Build it back in layers

      Base coat, mesh, finish — using materials rated to −40 °C, applied in the sequence and cure times the system was designed around.

    6. 06

      Match, then clean

      Colour and texture matched by fog coat or custom‑tinted acrylic so the repair disappears. Then the site goes back the way we found it, and we walk it with you.

    Calgary's exterior work window is shorter than most of Canada's. Booking early in the season is the difference between this year and next.
