The cement coat on exposed foundation between ground level and your siding. It takes more standing water and road salt than any other surface on the building, and it fails in sheets when the last coat went on over a dirty surface.

Woodpecker repair

Damaged foam and mesh are cut out and rebuilt rather than filled, then deterrents are fitted so the bird does not reopen the same elevation. Both halves are required, and the second is the one most contractors skip.

What goes wrong, and what it means

  • Cracks that grow each springFreeze and thaw cycling lets meltwater expand in hairline cracks, so repair them before the next winter.
  • Cracks at window cornersRapid temperature swings stress the weakest point at an opening, so monitor the crack and repair it if it widens.
  • Pockmarks on one elevationHail impact can chip acrylic finish and puncture EIFS foam, so inspect the wall after any major storm.
  • Dark streaks below windowsWind driven rain can enter failed flashing and window joints, so investigate dark streaks promptly.
  • Round holes high on the wallWoodpecker activity targets hollow-sounding EIFS walls, so rebuild the damage and install deterrents together.
  • White powder near ground levelMoisture moving through masonry carries mineral salt to the surface, so find the water source before cleaning the mark.

A‑03Wall systems
Assembly

What is behind
a stucco wall

Repair cost comes down to which layer failed and how far the damage travelled. Select a layer. Switch between the two systems used on Alberta homes.

Outside Inside

    FactorTraditional stuccoEIFS
    Build upSand, cement, lime and water over wire lath, in three coatsRigid foam board, mesh reinforced base coat, thin acrylic finish
    Upfront costLower material cost, higher labourHigher, from insulation board and specialised install
    InsulationNone on its ownContinuous foam layer, real energy saving
    Impact, hailDense and rigid, resists hail wellSoft substrate, dents and punctures
    MoistureSlightly porous, trapped moisture can escapeNeeds a drainage cavity. Older barrier systems trap water
    WoodpeckersRarely targetedSounds hollow, so a frequent target

    If your home has EIFS: confirm any repair uses a modern drainage style assembly rather than an outdated barrier system. A patch that seals water inside the wall causes more damage than the hole it covered.

    A working EIFS wall, three real layers deep. Watch it open up, then try each repair. Click anywhere on the wall to move the bird.

    Holes opened

    Watch the wall, or use the controls.

    The bird is a Northern Flicker, the species responsible for most stucco damage on the prairies. It is not hunting insects in the foam. It is drilling a nest cavity, and it chose your wall because the hollow sound matches a dead tree.

    That is why a cosmetic patch fails. The wall sounds identical afterwards, so the bird returns, usually within a few metres of the original hole. A real repair rebuilds the foam and mesh and changes what the wall tells the bird.

    A‑06Process
    Sequence

    How a job runs

    Stage two matters most. A repair that treats the symptom rather than the cause tends to return within a year or two.

    1. 01

      Assessment

      Free on site visit. We read the crack pattern, check flashing, caulking and gutters, and probe for moisture behind the finish.

    2. 02

      Diagnosis

      Drainage, flashing, foundation movement or original installation. We identify which before quoting.

    3. 03

      Written quote

      Scope, materials, timeline and payment schedule on paper before work begins. Free, no obligation.

    4. 04

      Preparation

      Loose material removed to sound substrate, damaged sheathing repaired, surfaces cleaned so new work bonds.

    5. 05

      Application

      Base coat, mesh and finish in sequence with proper cure times, using materials rated to minus forty.

    6. 06

      Match & clean

      Colour and texture matched by fog coat or tinted acrylic. Site cleaned daily, then walked with you.

    Questions

    Straight answers

    How much does stucco repair cost?
    There is no honest flat rate, because four things drive the price: the size of the damaged area, whether the wall is traditional stucco or EIFS, how hard the elevation is to reach, and how far the damage has travelled behind the finish. That last one cannot be judged from a photograph, which is why we assess on site first, and why the assessment is free.
    Can cracked stucco be repaired, or does the whole wall need redoing?
    Most cracks are repairable. Full replacement only becomes the better option when the same underlying fault has been running for several seasons, because by then the damage behind the finish is usually wider than anything visible from the street. We tell you which of the two you are looking at before you commit to anything.
    Do you charge for quotes?
    No. The visit, the assessment and the written quote are free and carry no obligation. The quote lists scope, materials, timeline and payment schedule, so you can put it side by side with anyone else's on the same terms.
    Can woodpecker damage to EIFS actually be fixed?
    Yes, but not by filling the hole. We cut back to sound material, rebuild the foam and mesh, then fit deterrents. Skipping that last step is why so many of these repairs fail. A rebuilt wall still sounds hollow, so the bird comes back to within a few metres of where it started.
    How fast can you come out after a hailstorm?
    Storm damage goes to the front of the queue, and we can usually inspect within a few days. A storm that crosses the whole region stretches that for every contractor at once, so it is worth calling early rather than waiting to see whether it settles.
    Do you work on acreages and rural properties?
    Yes. Rural work is routine for us across Rocky View, Foothills and Mountain View counties, and that includes sheds, garages and outbuildings rather than only the house.
    When should I book?
    Earlier than most people expect. Cement based products will not cure properly below a certain temperature, so Alberta has a shorter window for exterior work than most of the country. Once that window fills, the next opening is the following year.
    What should I ask any contractor before hiring them?
    Five things, and you should ask us the same five.
    • Proof of liability insurance and WCB coverage.
    • How much of their work has been done in Calgary freeze and thaw conditions, and whether they handle both traditional stucco and EIFS.
    • Recent local jobs you can go and look at yourself.
    • A written quote covering scope, materials, timeline, payment schedule and warranty.
    • How they intend to find the cause, rather than cover the symptom.
